After-school energy

Power nap20 min max · before 4 pm
20:00

Set a phone alarm as backup. The timer can't ring while the screen is locked.

  1. Keep your bed for night sleep. A long nap late in the day uses up the sleep drive your body built up, so you're wide awake at bedtime.
  2. Take 10 minutes outside when you get home. Daylight is the strongest signal that tells your body clock to stay awake.
  3. Have a protein snack and some water, then go straight into practice. Being upright and busy beats the crash.
  4. Keep the same bedtime and wake time every day, weekends included (within about an hour). Teens need 8–10 hours. AASM
  5. If you're still exhausted for weeks on 8+ hours of sleep, talk to a parent and a doctor. Sleep apnea and low iron are common causes.

Plans, pairings and why this works

If-then plans: tie each quest to something you already do. Your plan shows up under that quest.

Pairing: pick something you love that you only get during practice (a playlist, a podcast, a snack). Only during practice.

  1. Plans like "after X, I'll do Y" made reaching goals much more likely across 94 studies (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006).
  2. Pairing a treat with a chore: people allowed audiobooks only at the gym went 51% more often (Milkman et al. 2014, Management Science).
  3. Broken streaks lower motivation, and being able to repair them helps, so this app gives you streak shields (Silverman & Barasch 2023, J Consumer Research).
  4. People speed up as they get close to a goal, so the app shows how many quests you have left (Kivetz et al. 2006, J Marketing Research).
  5. Choosing your own reason (autonomy), seeing yourself improve (competence) and having people with you (relatedness) are what drive lasting motivation (Ryan & Deci 2000, Self-Determination Theory).
  6. People are more motivated by a new start (a Monday, the 1st of a month) than by a random day (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014).
  7. Game features like points and levels have a small real effect on motivation (g = 0.36), and work best when you combine competing with teaming up (Sailer & Homner 2020).
